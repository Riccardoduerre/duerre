Natural light changes throughout the day, but knowing the time is only part of the picture. To choose an exposure and composition, look at **where the light comes from, how large it is, and what it does to the materials in the scene**.

## Read the shadows

Shadow direction reveals where the light source sits. Long, defined shadows suggest direct light; softer edges can indicate a larger source, cloud cover, or reflected light. A pale wall, curtain, or reflector can bounce light back into a shadow without removing all the depth.

Move around the subject before changing your settings. A few steps can turn flat, frontal light into side light that describes volume and texture more clearly.

## Expose for what you want to keep

In a high-contrast scene, the sky and subject may need different compromises. Check the histogram and highlights, especially on skin, reflective surfaces, and pale fabrics. If you want to preserve detail in bright areas, expose accordingly and consider how far you can lift the shadows in post without introducing noise.

White balance needs the same attention. A nearby coloured wall can tint reflected light; open shade and direct sun can have very different colour temperatures. A neutral reference frame helps maintain continuity across a series.

## Adapt your approach to the scene

A window can provide soft, directional light; outdoors, a cloud can work as a diffuser. In harsh sun, look for open shade or use a bounce surface to control contrast. If your subject is moving, choose a shutter speed that keeps it sharp before raising the ISO.

There is no single correct setting. Start with the effect you want, read the available light, and change one variable at a time. That makes it easier to see which choice actually improved the photograph.
